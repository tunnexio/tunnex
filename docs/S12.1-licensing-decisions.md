# S12.1 — the licensing model. Commit-one.

**Status: FOUNDER-RULED AND LOCKED (2026-08-06).** The tier table, the gate list, the degradation rules and
the architecture are decided. What is open is listed in §8 as decide-items, and one of them **blocks the
build**.

This paper is the record. Product code follows it, not the other way round.

---

# 1. ⭐ THE RULING THAT EXPLAINS EVERY OTHER ONE: ADOPTION IS THE FIRST PRIORITY

> ## **THE MOAT IS COMMUNITY'S GENEROSITY, NOT ENTERPRISE'S LENGTH.**

Every question of the form *"could this move to Enterprise?"* was answered **no**, and the reason is one
sentence rather than a case-by-case judgement: **a thinner Community loses to NetBird**, whose self-hosted
edition is free with unlimited users. A feature list that wins an internal argument about value capture and
loses the deployment never gets to capture anything.

**So the full Zero Trust engine, AI agents, Kubernetes, OpenVPN, device posture, device approval, MFA
enforcement AND admin reset, Access Events and the full-retention audit log are all in Community.** Not as a
concession, and not "for now" — as the strategy.

⚠ **This is written down because it is the ruling most likely to be re-litigated by a future reader looking
at the tier table and seeing revenue left on the floor.** The floor is the point. Anyone proposing to move a
capability out of Community is proposing to change *this* ruling, and owes an argument against NetBird's
free tier, not an argument about that capability's worth.

# 1a. ⛔ REGISTER ROW — RANKED ABOVE THE BANDS, AND IT IS NOT A LICENSING QUESTION

> ## **DOES THE APACHE-2.0 RELICENSING HAPPEN BEFORE OR AFTER THE COPYRIGHT ASSIGNMENT — AND DOES THE
> ## ANSWER CHANGE WHAT THE ASSIGNMENT MUST COVER?**

**The facts, which are not in dispute:**

- **Copyright sits with the individual authors today.** There is no entity to hold it.
- **Relicensing is an act performed by the OWNER.** §6.3 moves 1,784 lines of the policy engine from
  proprietary source-available to Apache-2.0 — and under Apache-2.0 it cannot be withdrawn from anyone who
  received it.
- **So doing it before entity formation means the INDIVIDUALS perform it**, and the assignment to the
  entity then happens afterwards, **on code that has already been released**.
- ⚠ **And the assignment surface GROWS WITH EVERY COMMIT. The cost of waiting is not flat.**

⛔ **THE QUESTION IS RECORDED, NOT ANSWERED. NEITHER THE FOUNDER NOR I IS QUALIFIED TO RULE IT.** It is a
legal ordering question and it wants a lawyer, not a session. Nothing in this repository — no slice, no
refactor, no clever sequencing of commits — makes it answerable from here, and an answer produced by
reasoning about it in a session would carry exactly the confidence of a guess and none of the standing.

⭐ **WHAT THIS ROW IS FOR:** making sure the sequence is **CHOSEN DELIBERATELY** rather than **ARRIVED AT BY
WHICHEVER HAPPENS FIRST.** Both orderings are reachable by simply carrying on working — relicense lands
when the Community slice lands, assignment lands when the entity does — and the default is therefore
"whichever we get to first", which is not a decision. **The failure mode is not picking wrong; it is never
having picked.**

⚠ **Ranked above the bands** because the bands are revisable — a price is one line in a paper and one line
in a key — and **an Apache-2.0 release is not.** This is the only item in this document that cannot be
undone.

# 3. ⛔ WHY THERE IS NO USER COUNTING, DEVICE COUNTING, OR SEAT GATE ANYWHERE IN CODE

Three arguments, and they are independent — each would be sufficient.

## 3.1 It breaks the honest customer and does nothing to the dishonest one

A seat limit is enforced against the customer who **reports accurately**. The one who does not is
unaffected by it, because nothing in a self-hosted deployment can verify a headcount. **The gate lands
entirely on the population that was already going to pay.**

## 3.2 ⭐ A LIMIT THAT BLOCKS A NEW GATEWAY BLOCKS *GROWTH*. A LIMIT THAT BLOCKS A USER BLOCKS *WORK*

This is the load-bearing distinction and it is why the two look similar and are not.

Hitting the gateway limit means **the deployment wants to expand** — a new office, a new cloud, a second
site. That is a purchase conversation arriving at the moment its value is self-evident, and **nobody is
locked out of anything while it happens**: every existing gateway keeps running, every existing user keeps
working.

Hitting a seat limit means **somebody cannot do their job today**. A new hire cannot connect. The person
who triggers it is not the buyer, has no way to resolve it, and experiences the product as broken. **The
two limits feel identical on a pricing page and are opposite in the building.**

## 3.3 ⭐ AND A `nodes` ROW HAS EXACTLY ONE DEFINITION. A USER COUNT DOES NOT

A gateway is one row in `nodes`. There is no second opinion about whether it exists.

**"How many users" has no such answer, and the schema proves it.** `users` (`0002_tenancy.up.sql:26-37`)
carries `status IN ('active','deactivated')` **and** a nullable `deleted_at` **and** a nullable
`password_hash` (SSO-only accounts) — three independent axes before anyone asks about membership.
Membership is separate again: a user may belong to several orgs, or none.

**`CountOwners` is the proof that this is not hypothetical.** `queries/memberships.sql:47-56` needed *two*
queries for one question — a plain count for owners in an org, and `CountOrgsWhereSoleOwner` with a
**correlated subquery across every org the user belongs to**, carrying a `lint:cross-org` exemption, just to
answer *"is this person the last owner anywhere."*

> ## **IF COUNTING OWNERS HONESTLY TOOK A CORRELATED SUBQUERY AND A LINT EXEMPTION, A BILLING-GRADE USER
> ## COUNT IS NOT A `count(*)` — IT IS A POLICY DECISION WEARING A NUMBER'S CLOTHES.**

⚠ And every one of those axes is a place where a defect becomes **a customer wrongly blocked from
working**. Compare a gateway count, which cannot be wrong.

# 4. DEGRADATION

| state | behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| **valid** | everything works |
| **expired** | ⛔ **nothing stops.** A warning is shown |
| **90-day grace** | everything keeps working. ⛔ **New principals cannot enrol — existing ones returning still can** |
| **after grace** | the gated capabilities stop. ⛔ **The VPN does not** |

Three absolutes, and they are absolutes rather than defaults:

> ## ⛔ **A RUNNING VPN NEVER STOPS.**
> ## ⛔ **NO HUMAN IS EVER BLOCKED.**
> ## ⛔ **RUNNING GATEWAYS ARE NEVER STOPPED — THE LIMIT APPLIES AT ENROLMENT ONLY.**

## ⛔ "GATED CAPABILITIES STOP" WAS WRITTEN ABOUT CAPABILITIES THAT **GRANT**

**Amended on the D1 ruling (2026-08-06), because leaving the contradiction unstated is how a future reader
"fixes" it in the wrong direction.**

A licensing gate stops a capability the customer BOUGHT. Some of what a paid feature does is not a
capability in that sense at all — it is the product not leaking access. **IdP directory sync is both**: it
provisions joiners (the capability, sold) and it removes leavers (a revocation mechanism, not sold).

> ## ⭐ **A LICENCE MAY STOP GRANTING ACCESS. IT MUST NEVER STOP REMOVING IT.**

So after grace, IdP sync **does not fully stop**, and that is correct rather than an exception. The
reconciler keeps running and applies removals and deprovisions; only provisioning stops.

⚠ **This reads against the table above, and the TABLE is the thing that bends.** Anyone who finds this
inconsistency and "corrects" it by stopping the reconciler will have reintroduced the fail-open that D1
exists to close — membership frozen at the last sync, a directory-removed person keeping access
indefinitely. **The rule is: a revocation mechanism is not a gated capability, and never stops.**

Filed as a law in its own right, because it generalises past this feature: `docs/laws.md` →
*"downgrade releases enforcement" is a law about RESTRICTIONS.*

**The gateway limit is therefore a check in the enrolment path, and nowhere else.** This mirrors the org cap,
which is already built this way and is the precedent: `tenancy/service.go:73` checks **only** inside
`CreateOrganization`. Nothing is retroactive; existing orgs keep working forever. **The org cap is the shape
every other gate copies.**

⚠ **"New principals cannot enrol" needs a precise subject, and it is decide-item D3 (§8).** A device, an
agent and a gateway are all enrolments; a *returning* device is not a new principal. The distinction is
already in the schema (a device row exists or it does not), but the rule must name which verbs it binds.

# 4a. THE THREE LIFECYCLES — THE DEGRADATION RULES MADE CONCRETE

§4 states the rules. This states what they look like to an actual customer, because a table of states is
not a thing anyone can check their own deployment against.

## 4a.1 Community → Enterprise

**Free on one gateway, indefinitely.** Not a trial that expires into nothing, and not a crippled build.

⭐ **THE TRIGGER IS A SECOND SITE OR AN OUTAGE — NEVER A FEATURE WALL.** The customer does not upgrade
because they hit a locked screen; they upgrade because they opened another office, or moved into a second
cloud, or lost a gateway and wanted a standby. **The purchase conversation arrives at the moment its value
is self-evident**, which is the entire argument of §3.2 expressed as a sales motion.

30-day trial → **Starter** → **Growth** as sites grow.

## 4a.2 Community → Enterprise → Community (the lapse)

The path that matters most, because it is the one a customer fears and the one a competitor will ask about.

| point | what happens |
| --- | --- |
| **expiry** | ⛔ **nothing stops.** A warning appears |
| **90-day grace** | everything works. Only **new gateways and new orgs** are refused |
| **after grace** | SSO stops. IdP sync's **additive** half stops; **removals keep running** (D1) |

> ## ⭐ **AND ALL FIVE GATEWAYS KEEP WORKING — SITE-TO-SITE, CROSS-SITE DNS AND HA INCLUDED. A LAPSED
> ## CUSTOMER DOES NOT FALL BACK TO ONE GATEWAY. THEY SIMPLY STOP GROWING.**

That follows from §2.2 rather than being a separate promise: those capabilities were never gated, because
the gateway count already closed them. **A fleet that is already built stays built.**

⚠ **ONE CONSEQUENCE AN OPERATOR MUST NOT DISCOVER AT GRACE-END: the SSO lapse is a mass password-reset
event.** JIT-provisioned users are created with `PasswordHash: nil` (`sso/service.go:135`) and login refuses
a nil hash (`auth/service.go:137-140`). They are not locked out — `RequestPasswordReset`
(`auth/service.go:214-222`) does not check the hash — **but every one of them must complete a reset, on the
same day, and that requires working email delivery.** For a fifty-person org that is fifty resets at once.

⛔ **Documented for the OPERATOR in `docs/licensing.md`, not only here** — with the mitigation ordered:
renew first; if lapsing deliberately, verify email delivery and have people set a password WHILE SSO STILL
WORKS, which takes the event off the critical day. ⚠ And the line that is easy to miss: **administrators are
users too** — if every admin is SSO-only, they are in the affected group as well.

A consequence that lives in a decisions paper is a consequence nobody affected will read.

## 4a.3 Community only — ⭐ NOT A FAILURE

The deployment that never pays is **not a loss and must never be designed against.**

It is adoption, it is contributions, and it is a pipeline. **Today's eight-person team is tomorrow's
eighty — and by then they are already a user**, running the product in production, with their policies
written and their gateways enrolled. The competitor they would otherwise have evaluated at eighty people
never gets a hearing.

⚠ **This is §1 restated as an operational rule:** any proposal that makes the never-paying deployment worse
is trading a certain adoption cost against a speculative conversion gain, and §1 already ruled on which of
those wins.

# 4b. TWO FINDINGS FROM WRITING THE OPERATOR DOC, BOTH LARGER THAN THE DOC

Written down because each is a defect class, not a paragraph of copy.

## 4b.1 ⭐ "ADMINISTRATORS ARE USERS TOO" IS THE `CountOwners` SHAPE IN A NEW DOMAIN

`docs/licensing.md` warns that if every administrator in an org is SSO-only, they are in the mass
password-reset group as well — **and an org that misses this has nobody who can act.**

> ## ⛔ **THE INVARIANT LOOKS SATISFIED AND NOBODY CAN SIGN IN.**

The org has owners. The owner count is correct. Every membership row is exactly as it should be — **and the
capability the invariant exists to guarantee is absent**, because the constraint was written about *roles*
and the failure lands on *credentials*.

⚠ **That is precisely why `CountOwners` needed `CountOrgsWhereSoleOwner` beside it** (§3.3): counting the
role never answered the question the role exists to protect. **Same shape, new domain — and it arrived
through a licensing lapse, which is not where anyone would have gone looking for it.**

## 4b.2 ⛔ D1'S FAIL-OPEN IS REACHABLE THROUGH THE CONSOLE, NOT ONLY THROUGH CODE

`docs/licensing.md` states that *"directory sync is partially licensed"* is **not an error**, and that
disconnecting or re-entering the credential to "fix" it is what would stop the removals.

**That is the same fail-open D1 closed, reached by a different actor.** The code cannot cause it any more —
the copy guard rejects fault vocabulary and the reconciler has no seam on the removal path. **But an
operator can still do it to themselves**, by taking correct remedial action against a message they
reasonably misread.

> ## ⭐ **TWO HALVES OF ONE DEFECT, AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS CODE.** The guard stops us from causing it; the
> ## documentation stops the operator from causing it. Fixing either half alone leaves the hole open.

⚠ **The general form, which is the part worth carrying:** when a mitigation's failure mode is *"a human
does the sensible thing"*, the code fix is not the whole fix, and a test suite will report the class as
closed. **Ask who else can reach the state, not only what else can write to it.**

# 5. ARCHITECTURE — ENTITLEMENTS, NOT EDITIONS

⛔ **Named constants plus ONE map, tier → feature set, as DATA.**

```go
license.Has(FeatSSO)      // yes
if edition == "enterprise" // ⛔ never
```

> ## ⭐ **MOVING A FEATURE BETWEEN TIERS IS ONE LINE IN THAT MAP.**

That property is the whole reason for the shape. §1 says adoption is the priority and the tier table may be
revised in either direction as the market answers; **a model whose tier boundary is scattered across
`if edition ==` branches cannot be revised, it can only be rewritten.** Today's boundary is 25 files and 40
web call sites (§7.1) — the cost of having encoded a boundary as control flow instead of data.

**Three properties the map must have:**

1. **One definition.** The tier→feature map is the single source of the boundary. A capability's tier is
   read from it, never restated at a call site.
2. **Feature constants are named per capability**, never reused — the same rule RBAC permissions already
   follow, for the same reason: a shared name makes two capabilities impossible to separate later.
3. **The map is enumerable at runtime**, because the census (§9) reads it and because an operator has to be
   able to see what their key entitles them to without reading source.

**"The whole product, one gateway" is a true description of a product that has not been assembled yet.**
Not a reason to change the model — a reason not to describe S12.1 as repackaging.

## 6.2 ⚠ THE ORG CAP'S SHAPE EXISTS; ITS INPUT DOES NOT

`tenancy/service.go:73-82` is live and correct, and `CountOrganizations`
(`queries/organizations.sql:27-29`) is deployment-global (`deleted_at IS NULL`), which matches the row.

⛔ **But `enterprise.Unlimited` is a compile-time `const`** (`edition_open.go`: `const Unlimited = false`).
`if !enterprise.Unlimited` is a constant branch the compiler eliminates — in the enterprise binary the check
**is not present**, rather than being present-and-false. Converting it to a runtime read is a real change.

⚠ **And its user-visible prose breaks.** `service.go:80` says *"the open edition supports a single
organization; upgrade to Tunnex Enterprise"*. Under a license model there is no "open edition."

✅ **S4.7's reactive-403 funnel survives**, because it branches on the **code**, not the prose:
`CreateOrg.tsx:79-84` keys on `org_limit_reached` and re-checks memberships before showing the dead-end.
**Keep the error code identical** and the funnel needs no change.

## 6.3 ⛔ RELICENSING — A ONE-WAY DOOR, AND IT IS LEGAL RATHER THAN TECHNICAL

`apps/api/internal/enterprise/LICENSE:1-6` governs *"the contents of this directory and any code compiled
only under the `enterprise` build tag."* Measured footprint:

| --- | --- |

> ## ⛔ **ONCE RELEASED UNDER APACHE-2.0 IT CANNOT BE WITHDRAWN FROM ANYONE WHO RECEIVED IT.**

Not a reason to stop — §1 already rules that these belong in Community. It is recorded so the irreversibility

# 7. CORRECTIONS CARRIED FROM THE REVIEW

Both were on the founder's own gate list and were removed by the founder after measurement. Recorded with
their reasons, per the rejected-alternatives convention.

## 7.1 ⛔ AUDIT-LOG RETENTION — DROPPED

The proposal was 30 days in Community, unlimited in Enterprise. **Dropped for two independent reasons.**

**(a) It cannot be built without removing the audit log's immutability.** `0002_tenancy.up.sql:109-115`
installs three triggers — `audit_logs_no_update`, `audit_logs_no_delete`, `audit_logs_no_truncate` — all
raising *"audit_logs is append-only: % is not permitted"* (`:105`). TRUNCATE is blocked **separately**, with
the comment *"statement-level and bypasses row triggers — block it too"*: someone already closed the obvious
way around. The contrast is the proof it was deliberate — `access_events` has **no** such trigger, which is
exactly why S7.5.1 could give it a retention sweep (`queries/access_events.sql:44-77`).

⚠ **And the trigger is table-wide, not tier-aware.** Weakening it for Community weakens it for Enterprise.

**(b) ⛔ It is the only proposed gate whose enforcement is IRREVERSIBLE.** Every other gate releases by
turning something back on. This one **deletes rows**. A customer who lapses, gets swept, then pays, does not
get their history back.

> ## **A PRICING LEVER THAT DESTROYS CUSTOMER DATA ON NON-PAYMENT IS A DIFFERENT CATEGORY OF THING FROM
> ## "SSO STOPS WORKING."**

**Community ships the audit log at full retention.**

## 7.2 HA / FAILOVER — DROPPED FROM THE GATE LIST

Not because it is unimportant, but because §2.2 already closes it: `failover.go:239,278` guard on
`len(configured) >= 2`. **Gating it separately would be the second truth §2.2 forbids.**

⚠ It is also worth recording what a separate gate *would* have been: **a taking-away.** `nodes/failover.go`
carries no build tag and the hub-set read authorizes on `PermOrgView` alone (`site_handlers.go:346`) — HA is
**shipped and ungated today**. The gateway cap removes it from single-gateway deployments as a consequence;
a dedicated gate would have removed it from people who have it, which is a different act.

| **D4** | Gateway bands and their prices | **FOUNDER'S. Not ours to rule** |
| **D5** | Entity formation (gates Support/SLA) | **FOUNDER'S. Not ours to unblock** |

**D2, stated so it is not discovered during a lapse:** there is no `sso_only` flag, so password login is never
disabled — but JIT-provisioned users are created with `PasswordHash: nil` (`sso/service.go:135`) and login
refuses a nil hash (`auth/service.go:137-140`). `RequestPasswordReset` does **not** check the hash
(`auth/service.go:214-222`), so they can set one. **Net: nobody is permanently locked out, but every
JIT-provisioned user must complete a password reset, which requires working email delivery.** For a
fifty-person org that is a mass password-reset event on the day grace ends. It does not violate "no human is
ever blocked" — it is a lot of friction that ought to be a deliberate choice.
